# Original-Rounding Power

Implementation revision 6 implements [POW-01](../../../../../formulations/openifs/support/scalar_power.md) through the unique entry `md2asm_pow_original_rounding_candidate`. FP64 is the 64-bit floating-point format. The arguments are numerical values $`x`$ and $`y`$, and the result approximates $`x^y`$. Callers define physical units. This direct body preserves the frozen GLIBC 2.38 boundary at commit `36f2487f13e3540be9ee0fb51876b1da72176d3f`, including its exact return word, cumulative arithmetic status and `errno`, the system error indicator.

The [parent](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_original_rounding.S), [integer child](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_integer_rounding.S) and [coefficient owners](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_coefficient_tables.S) form one direct assembly implementation. The LLM emitted their instructions from the equations and frozen source dependency order. Compiler assembly and intermediate representations did not supply the body. The coefficient words retain the already rounded source bytes. GNU-derived algorithm and data retain the LGPL-2.1-or-later notice.

## Frozen Calculation

Let $`R(q)`$ mean one nearest-even rounding of $`q`$ to FP64. Ordinary products, sums, subtractions and divisions each retain their separate rounding. Let $`S(a,b,c)=R(ab+c)`$ mean one rounding of the exact product plus addend inside this mathematical boundary. Twenty source locations define this boundary $`S`$; branches determine which locations run. The ordinary path executes nineteen boundaries. At three sites, a proved exact multiplication lets separate hardware multiplication and addition supply the same effective rounding. Other sites retain the integer child. Primary weather arithmetic keeps its declared separate rounding points.

For a finite positive magnitude, integer bit operations write $`x=2^kz`$ exactly and choose one of 128 logarithm records. Each record contains a reciprocal centre $`v`$, its logarithm $`c`$ and a small logarithm remainder $`c_t`$. The original word offset is `0x3fe6955500000000`; extracting seven index bits gives a bounded index from 0 to 127. The constants $`L_h,L_l`$ split $`\ln(2)`$, and $`A_0,\ldots,A_6`$ are fixed polynomial coefficients. Evaluate the following dependencies in order.

```math
r=S(z,v,-1),\quad t_1=S(k,L_h,c),\quad t_2=R(t_1+r),
\ell_1=S(k,L_l,c_t),\quad \ell_2=R(R(t_1-t_2)+r),
a=R(A_0r),\quad a_2=R(ra),\quad a_3=R(ra_2),\quad H=R(t_2+a_2),
\ell_3=S(a,r,-a_2),\quad \ell_4=R(R(t_2-H)+a_2),
p_6=S(r,A_6,A_5),\quad p_4=S(r,A_4,A_3),\quad p_2=S(r,A_2,A_1),
u=S(a_2,p_6,p_4),\quad p=S(a_2,u,p_2),
w=R(R(R(\ell_1+\ell_2)+\ell_3)+\ell_4),\quad \ell=S(a_3,p,w),
L=R(H+\ell),\quad L_t=R(R(H-L)+\ell).
```

Here $`L+L_t`$ retains the small remainder of the logarithm. Form $`e_h=R(yL)`$ and $`e_l=S(y,L_t,S(y,L,-e_h))`$. The exponential reduction multiplies $`e_h`$ by the fixed inverse logarithm factor, then uses `FRINTA` and `FCVTAS` for the original nearest-integer index with ties away from zero. This index rule remains distinct from nearest-even FP64 arithmetic. With the rounded index $`d`$, the two negative logarithm constants $`n_h,n_l`$ and the selected exponential table remainder $`t`$, form

```math
r=R(S(d,n_l,S(d,n_h,e_h))+e_l),\quad r_2=R(rr),
p_{23}=S(r,C_3,C_2),\quad p_{45}=S(r,C_5,C_4),
b=S(r_2,p_{23},R(t+r)),\quad q=S(R(r_2r_2),p_{45},b).
```

The ordinary result is $`S(s,q,s)`$, where $`s`$ is the signed table scale. Large positive exponents adjust that scale by $`2^{-1009}`$, apply $`S`$, then multiply by $`2^{1009}`$. Small results first increase the scale by $`2^{1022}`$. Their correction preserves the original separate product and sum, retains a high part and remainder before scaling by $`2^{-1022}`$, fixes a rounded zero's sign, and executes the original explicit underflow operation. Tiny exponential arguments return the signed rounded value $`R(1+e_h)`$. The assembly retains all branch thresholds and operation order.

## Exact Integer Child

A finite FP64 input has the exact form $`(-1)^sM2^E`$. Its sign is $`s`$. For a normal number, the integer significand $`M`$ includes the implicit leading bit and $`E`$ is the stored exponent minus 1075. For a zero or subnormal number, $`M`$ is the stored fraction and $`E=-1074`$. The child forms the exact product and addend on the shared scale $`2^{-2148}`$.

Two arrays of 67 unsigned 64-bit words hold these exact magnitudes, with the lowest word first. Their 4288-bit capacity exceeds the required 4197-bit bound for every finite input and its possible sum. Equal signs select addition with carry between words. Different signs select magnitude comparison followed by subtraction with borrow. The result replaces the product array, so every read stays within the two complete arrays.

For a normal result, retain the highest 53 bits. For a subnormal result, shift by 1074 bits on the fixed integer scale. The first discarded bit locates halfway, while all lower bits distinguish a value above halfway. Round upward above halfway, or at halfway when the retained integer is odd. Exact cancellation returns positive zero. Two zero terms return negative zero only when both term signs are negative. A zero product with a nonzero addend returns that addend unchanged.

The child adds inexact to the floating-point status register, FPSR when discarded bits are nonzero. A tiny inexact value before rounding also adds underflow, including a value that rounds to the minimum normal number. Overflow adds overflow and inexact and returns the signed infinity. Incoming flags remain set, and `errno` stays unchanged. Nonfinite internal operands stop through `_exit(116)`. The fast and wide diagnostic counters retain the frozen operand classification; the [compact exact path](compact_product_sum.md) and then the [wide exact path](wide_product_sum.md) handle their proved subsets before the unchanged bounded word calculation. Each child refusal keeps its floating-point inputs and status intact.

## Exact Products At Split Coefficients

The logarithm boundary $`S(k,L_h,c)`$ uses a coefficient with 42 significant binary digits. The integer $`k`$ lies from minus 1074 through 1024, so its magnitude uses at most eleven binary digits. Their product therefore fits exactly in FP64, which has 53 significant binary digits. Separate `FMUL` and `FADD` instructions preserve $`S`$ because the multiplication requires no rounding and raises no exception flag. The addition performs the original single effective rounding. A zero product retains the original signed-zero rule.

The exponential boundary $`S(d,n_h,e_h)`$ uses a coefficient with 36 significant binary digits. A runtime integer guard selects the same direct calculation when $`\lvert d\rvert<131072`$, so the integer uses at most seventeen binary digits. Larger indices retain the original integer child and pending-mask update. Every admitted product is normal or zero, and every nonzero exact sum is normal and finite. Here normal means that the FP64 value uses its full 53-bit significand, without the reduced precision of values very close to zero.

The low logarithm boundary $`S(k,L_l,c_t)`$ uses a coefficient with 49 significant binary digits. An integer guard admits zero, or an integer $`k`$ that uses at most four significant binary digits after all factors of two are removed. For example, 30 is $`15\times2`$ and passes, while 31 does not pass. The stored FP64 word passes this guard when its lowest 49 bits are zero. The admitted product is exact and raises no flag, so separate multiplication and addition again retain $`S`$. Other integers keep the original child and pending-mask update.

The original aligned integer magnitudes remain below 127 bits at all three admitted sites, so each direct route increments the original fast counter once, including unsigned wrap. Hardware collects the addition's flags, while earlier pending flags in `x27` remain intact until the original commit points. The wide counter, error indicator, stack frame, saved registers and all other arithmetic remain unchanged. The complete parent passes 11,041 immediate comparisons and 481 saved-register probes, including inherited flags, counter wrap and both signs and zero at the new guard edges. These focused checks add no independent global accuracy bound or intermediate forecast capture.

## Interface And Status

The AArch64 application binary interface passes the parent's operands in `d0` and `d1` and returns the result in `d0`. The child `md2asm_round_product_sum_candidate` takes `d0`, `d1` and `d2` and returns `d0`. Both require the floating-point control register, FPCR, to be zero, which selects nearest-even arithmetic and gradual underflow. They preserve `x18`, `x19` through `x29`, the low 64 bits of `v8` through `v15`, and sixteen-byte stack alignment. The parent has a 256-byte frame; the child uses a 16-byte return-address frame for each compact or wide call, or a separate 1728-byte general frame. Neither receives a numerical buffer.

Within the child's general frame, the two 67-word scratch arrays occupy bytes 0 through 1,071, counted from the stack pointer. Clear all 1,072 bytes with sixteen 64-byte groups and an exact 48-byte tail. Caller-saved `v3` holds zero for the stores, while `d0` through `d2` remain intact during clearing. No store reaches the saved-register region. The general arithmetic, fast/wide counter classification and frame sizes stay unchanged. The linked compact child skips a status-register write when every required exception bit is already set.

The read-only exponential owner `md2asm_original_pow_exp_data` contains 2160 bytes: fourteen header words and 256 table words. The logarithm owner `md2asm_original_pow_log_data` contains 4168 bytes: nine header words and 128 four-word records. Both start on a sixteen-byte boundary, and their seven-bit indices keep every load in bounds. Each exported diagnostic counter occupies one aligned eight-byte word. Diagnostic status-clear and status-read entries intentionally clear or read FPSR; numerical calls preserve cumulative status.

The parent retains the original branches for signed zeros, negative bases, integer exponents, infinities and NaNs, including the original signaling status and payload selection. Invalid finite powers set Linux `EDOM=33`; the original overflow, zero-underflow and division-by-zero routes set `ERANGE=34`. Their arithmetic sets the original flags before the C runtime `__errno_location` identifies the thread's error indicator. The runtime boundary preserves `x18`. The only other required service is `_exit`; the direct library has no C numerical fallback, libm or Fortran call.

## Private Status Collection

The parent uses a [private child](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_deferred_product_sum.S) to collect exception flags, such as inexact rounding, between internal calculations. Build the existing compact and wide sources with `MD2ASM_PRODUCT_SUM_DEFERRED_STATUS` to select their private names and mask-return tails. The numerical bodies are shared. Their ordinary public builds retain the same instructions and immediate status behaviour.

Private success returns the same result in `d0`, success zero in `w0` and a required flag mask of zero, 16 or 20 in `w1`. Refusal returns one in `w0`, preserving the floating inputs and hardware status. The leaves retain the same mode and finite-input guards, arithmetic, register obligations and bounds. The private wrapper retains the original fast/wide counter classification and increments. It borrows the parent's saved `x27` as a pending mask and preserves it. Unsupported cases commit the pending mask before calling the unchanged general assembly calculation, then return mask zero because that calculation updates hardware status itself. The extra general-call return-address frame is 16 bytes.

The parent starts its pending mask at zero and combines each returned mask with integer OR. Its ordinary floating-point operations continue to raise hardware flags naturally. At every public return, it adds the pending bits to the current hardware status without clearing any incoming or newly raised bit. Before the error-slot service, it commits and clears the pending copy so the service's returned status remains authoritative. The parent frame stays 256 bytes, with no new buffer, constant owner or numerical stage.

This private interval has no source status read, status clear, profiling callback or counter getter. FPCR zero disables numerical traps, and exception flags do not affect the later results. The contract preserves the public parent's result, status, error indicator and counters, and every public atomic interface. It does not promise identical status to an asynchronous observer inside the private interval. The independent mathematical and whole-forecast physical limits remain unchanged.

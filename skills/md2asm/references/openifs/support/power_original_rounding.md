# Original-Rounding Power

Implementation revision 11 implements [POW-01](../../../../../formulations/openifs/support/scalar_power.md) through the unique entry `md2asm_pow_original_rounding_candidate`. FP64 is the 64-bit floating-point format. The arguments are numerical values $`x`$ and $`y`$, and the result approximates $`x^y`$. Callers define physical units. This direct body preserves the frozen GLIBC 2.38 boundary at commit `36f2487f13e3540be9ee0fb51876b1da72176d3f`, including its exact return word, cumulative arithmetic status and `errno`, the system error indicator.

The [parent](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_original_rounding.S), [integer child](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_integer_rounding.S) and [coefficient owners](../../../../../src/openifs/support/power_coefficient_tables.S) form one direct assembly implementation. The LLM emitted their instructions from the equations and frozen source dependency order. Compiler assembly and intermediate representations did not supply the body. The coefficient words retain the already rounded source bytes. GNU-derived algorithm and data retain the LGPL-2.1-or-later notice.

## Frozen Calculation

Let $`R(q)`$ mean one nearest-even rounding of $`q`$ to FP64. Ordinary products, sums, subtractions and divisions each retain their separate rounding. Let $`S(a,b,c)=R(ab+c)`$ mean one rounding of the exact product plus addend inside this mathematical boundary. Twenty source locations define this boundary $`S`$; branches determine which locations run. The ordinary path executes nineteen boundaries. At three sites, a proved exact multiplication lets separate hardware multiplication and addition supply the same effective rounding. Other boundaries retain exact integer arithmetic. Primary weather arithmetic keeps its declared separate rounding points.

For a finite positive magnitude, integer bit operations write $`x=2^kz`$ exactly and choose one of 128 logarithm records. Each record contains a reciprocal centre $`v`$, its logarithm $`c`$ and a small logarithm remainder $`c_t`$. The original word offset is `0x3fe6955500000000`; extracting seven index bits gives a bounded index from 0 to 127. The constants $`L_h,L_l`$ split $`\ln(2)`$, and $`A_0,\ldots,A_6`$ are fixed polynomial coefficients. Evaluate the following dependencies in order.

```math
r=S(z,v,-1),\quad t_1=S(k,L_h,c),\quad t_2=R(t_1+r),
\ell_1=S(k,L_l,c_t),\quad \ell_2=R(R(t_1-t_2)+r),
a=R(A_0r),\quad a_2=R(ra),\quad a_3=R(ra_2),\quad H=R(t_2+a_2),
\ell_3=S(a,r,-a_2),\quad \ell_4=R(R(t_2-H)+a_2),
p_6=S(r,A_6,A_5),\quad p_4=S(r,A_4,A_3),\quad p_2=S(r,A_2,A_1),
u=S(a_2,p_6,p_4),\quad p=S(a_2,u,p_2),
w=R(R(R(\ell_1+\ell_2)+\ell_3)+\ell_4),\quad \ell=S(a_3,p,w),
L=R(H+\ell),\quad L_t=R(R(H-L)+\ell).
```

Here $`L+L_t`$ retains the small remainder of the logarithm. Form $`e_h=R(yL)`$ and $`e_l=S(y,L_t,S(y,L,-e_h))`$. The exponential reduction multiplies $`e_h`$ by the fixed inverse logarithm factor, then uses `FRINTA` and `FCVTAS` for the original nearest-integer index with ties away from zero. This index rule remains distinct from nearest-even FP64 arithmetic. With the rounded index $`d`$, the two negative logarithm constants $`n_h,n_l`$ and the selected exponential table remainder $`t`$, form

```math
r=R(S(d,n_l,S(d,n_h,e_h))+e_l),\quad r_2=R(rr),
p_{23}=S(r,C_3,C_2),\quad p_{45}=S(r,C_5,C_4),
b=S(r_2,p_{23},R(t+r)),\quad q=S(R(r_2r_2),p_{45},b).
```

The ordinary result is $`S(s,q,s)`$, where $`s`$ is the signed table scale. Large positive exponents adjust that scale by $`2^{-1009}`$, apply $`S`$, then multiply by $`2^{1009}`$. Small results first increase the scale by $`2^{1022}`$. Their correction preserves the original separate product and sum, retains a high part and remainder before scaling by $`2^{-1022}`$, fixes a rounded zero's sign, and executes the original explicit underflow operation. Tiny exponential arguments return the signed rounded value $`R(1+e_h)`$. The assembly retains all branch thresholds and operation order.

## Exact Integer Child

A finite FP64 input has the exact form $`(-1)^sM2^E`$. Its sign is $`s`$. For a normal number, the integer significand $`M`$ includes the implicit leading bit and $`E`$ is the stored exponent minus 1075. For a zero or subnormal number, $`M`$ is the stored fraction and $`E=-1074`$. The child forms the exact product and addend on the shared scale $`2^{-2148}`$.

Two arrays of 67 unsigned 64-bit words hold these exact magnitudes, with the lowest word first. Their 4288-bit capacity exceeds the required 4197-bit bound for every finite input and its possible sum. Equal signs select addition with carry between words. Different signs select magnitude comparison followed by subtraction with borrow. The result replaces the product array, so every read stays within the two complete arrays.

For a normal result, retain the highest 53 bits. For a subnormal result, shift by 1074 bits on the fixed integer scale. The first discarded bit locates halfway, while all lower bits distinguish a value above halfway. Round upward above halfway, or at halfway when the retained integer is odd. Exact cancellation returns positive zero. Two zero terms return negative zero only when both term signs are negative. A zero product with a nonzero addend returns that addend unchanged.

The child adds inexact to the floating-point status register, FPSR when discarded bits are nonzero. A tiny inexact value before rounding also adds underflow, including a value that rounds to the minimum normal number. Overflow adds overflow and inexact and returns the signed infinity. Incoming flags remain set, and `errno` stays unchanged. Nonfinite internal operands stop through `_exit(116)`. The fast and wide diagnostic counters retain the frozen operand classification; the [compact exact path](compact_product_sum.md) and then the [wide exact path](wide_product_sum.md) handle their proved subsets before the unchanged bounded word calculation. Each child refusal keeps its floating-point inputs and status intact.

## Exact Products At Split Coefficients

The logarithm boundary $`S(k,L_h,c)`$ uses a coefficient with 42 significant binary digits. The integer $`k`$ lies from minus 1074 through 1024, so its magnitude uses at most eleven binary digits. Their product therefore fits exactly in FP64, which has 53 significant binary digits. Separate `FMUL` and `FADD` instructions preserve $`S`$ because the multiplication requires no rounding and raises no exception flag. The addition performs the original single effective rounding. A zero product retains the original signed-zero rule.

The exponential boundary $`S(d,n_h,e_h)`$ uses a coefficient with 36 significant binary digits. A runtime integer guard selects the same direct calculation when $`\lvert d\rvert<131072`$, so the integer uses at most seventeen binary digits. Larger indices retain the original integer child and pending-mask update. Every admitted product is normal or zero, and every nonzero exact sum is normal and finite. Here normal means that the FP64 value uses its full 53-bit significand, without the reduced precision of values very close to zero.

The low logarithm boundary $`S(k,L_l,c_t)`$ uses a coefficient with 49 significant binary digits. An integer guard admits zero, or an integer $`k`$ that uses at most four significant binary digits after all factors of two are removed. For example, 30 is $`15\times2`$ and passes, while 31 does not pass. The stored FP64 word passes this guard when its lowest 49 bits are zero. The admitted product is exact and raises no flag, so separate multiplication and addition again retain $`S`$. Other integers keep the original child and pending-mask update.

The original aligned integer magnitudes remain below 127 bits at all three admitted sites, so each direct route increments the original fast counter once, including unsigned wrap. Hardware collects the addition's flags, while earlier pending flags in `x27` remain intact until the original commit points. The wide counter, error indicator, stack frame, saved registers and all other arithmetic remain unchanged.

## Bounded Integer Rounding

The logarithm reduction gives either zero or $`2^{-60}\le\lvert r\rvert<1/128`$. For nonzero $`r`$, its stored exponent $`e_r`$ is from 963 through 1015. A normal FP64 value has 53 significant binary digits, including its implicit leading digit. Write their integer value as $`M_r`$, so $`r=(-1)^sM_r2^{e_r-1075}`$, where $`s`$ is the sign bit. The complete reciprocal-table intervals establish this range for every valid parent input that reaches these sites. Recorded forecast values do not define the range.

For the square-product remainder $`S(a,r,-a_2)`$, the original coefficient gives $`a=-r/2`$ exactly and $`a_2=R(ar)`$. Let $`P=M_r^2`$ and $`E=2e_r-2151`$. The positive rounded magnitude $`-a_2`$ has integer significand $`C`$ and scale $`2^{E_c}`$. Its aligned integer value is $`C_I=C2^{E_c-E}`$. Nearest-even rounding gives

```math
D=C_I-P,\qquad \lvert D\rvert\le 2^{52},\qquad S(a,r,-a_2)=D2^E.
```

The low 64 bits of the product and aligned rounded value therefore contain the complete signed error. Integer subtraction recovers it without the high product word. `SCVTF` converts $`D`$ exactly, and multiplication by the normal power of two $`2^E`$ is also exact. Here $`-225\le E\le-121`$, so every nonzero result is normal and finite. Zero input and exact cancellation return positive zero. The required exception mask is zero and the original fast counter advances once. The original operands remain available for a complete replay when FPCR is nonzero.

The later remainder $`S(y,L,-e_h)`$, where $`e_h=R(yL)`$, uses the same small-error argument with two variable operands. Here let $`a=y`$, $`b=L`$ and $`c=-e_h`$. Write their exact significand product as $`P=M_aM_b`$ with scale $`E=e_a+e_b-2150`$. The rounded product has a 53-bit significand $`C`$ aligned by 52 or 53 bits. Subtracting that aligned value from the low product word recovers the complete signed integer error, whose magnitude is at most $`2^{52}`$. Correct its sign using the original product sign, then convert and scale exactly. A zero error returns positive zero.

Integer guards require normal finite operands and rounded addend, with $`E\ge-1022`$. The finite rounded product also gives $`E\le919`$, so the scale and every nonzero returned error are normal and finite. The required exception mask is zero and the original fast counter advances once. After the first operand has passed its normal finite guard, either signed-zero second operand also gives an exact positive-zero remainder. The raw operands and exception flags remain intact. Every unsupported mode, input or scale keeps the complete original assembly path with the raw operands intact.

The three polynomial seeds $`p_6,p_4,p_2`$ use fixed coefficient pairs with equal exponents and opposite signs. For each pair, let $`B,C`$ be the positive integer significands of its multiplier and addend. In units of the addend's scale, the exact magnitude is $`C-rB`$. Exact endpoint bounds keep this value strictly between $`2^{52}`$ and $`2^{53}`$ and more than one half-unit from either endpoint. The result therefore keeps the addend's sign and exponent, and adjacent stored results are one integer unit apart on this scale.

Let $`P=M_rB`$ and $`j=1075-e_r`$, so $`60\le j\le112`$. The integer quotient of $`P/2^j`$ and its first discarded bit determine the rounded offset. The three multiplier significands have zero, one and one trailing zero bits. Since $`M_r`$ has at most 52 trailing zero bits, a lower discarded bit is always nonzero. A nonzero input therefore has no exact or halfway case, and the required inexact mask is 16. Add the first discarded bit to the quotient, then subtract that rounded offset from $`C`$ for positive $`r`$ or add it for negative $`r`$. When $`j\ge107`$, the offset is below one half, so the original addend word is returned with the same inexact mask. Either signed zero returns the original addends with mask zero.

These three seeds are independent until their shared join. The private leaf returns their three separately rounded words together and combines their masks once. The original aligned width is $`53+j`$, so all three nonzero-input seeds retain the fast class when $`j\le74`$ and the wide class otherwise. Either signed zero retains the fast class. One increment by three preserves the original counter total, including unsigned wrap. The original three-call sequence supplies every nonzero-FPCR replay.

The triplet interface reads `d10` and the first three fixed coefficient records in a read-only 96-byte region at `x16`, aligned to at least eight bytes, and returns `d0`, `d1`, `d2`, `w1` for the mask and `w0` as zero for success or one for refusal. It changes only caller-saved registers, preserves `x18` through `x30` and all of `v8` through `v15`, including the pending mask in `x27`, and uses no stack or external call. The parent writes the three words at bytes 208, 216 and 224 of its existing 256-byte frame. The square and general product-remainder entries keep the original three-operand interface and preserve raw `d1` and `d2`; `d3` is temporary storage for the exact scale. On admitted paths, all three entries preserve FPCR, incoming FPSR and `errno` until the existing parent observation points. Nonzero-mode replay retains the original fallback status behaviour.

## Two Exponential Seeds And Ordered Logarithm Merges

The exponential seeds $`p_{23}`$ and $`p_{45}`$ are independent until their next consumer. Their private entry checks FPCR zero and either signed zero or the complete range $`2^{-60}\le\lvert r\rvert<1/128`$ before changing results, counters or status. For nonzero $`r`$, write $`r=\sigma M_r2^{-j}`$, with $`60\le j\le112`$ and sign $`\sigma`$ equal to plus or minus one. Let $`B_i,C_i`$ be the positive significands in the two original coefficient records. In the first and second addends' units, the exact values are $`C_1+\sigma M_rB_1/2^{j+1}`$ and $`C_2+\sigma M_rB_2/2^{j+2}`$. A lower discarded bit is always nonzero, so the quotient and first discarded bit determine rounding, with required inexact mask 16. Both signed zeros return the original addends with mask zero. For $`j\ge106`$, each nonzero change is below half an addend unit, so those original words also remain the rounded results.

The first positive seed can cross a power of two, where the spacing between stored results doubles. Here $`C_1=2^{53}-579`$. With $`Q=\lfloor M_rB_1/2^{j+1}\rfloor`$, the exact crossing occurs when $`Q\ge579`$. Floor brackets keep only the integer part. On that side, the correctly rounded coarse significand is $`\lfloor C_1/2\rfloor+\lfloor Q/2\rfloor+1`$. On the other side, ordinary fine-scale rounding can itself carry into the next exponent. The raw-word construction retains that carry. Each branch rounds the exact value once, including at the boundary. The second seed stays in its original interval between powers of two. The original counter classes are two fast calls for $`j\le72`$, fast then wide for $`j=73`$, and two wide calls for larger $`j`$; zeros remain fast. Each total retains unsigned wrap.

The pair reads `d10` and the final two coefficient records at `x16`. Two bounded sixteen-byte loads read the original significands inside their 64-byte region, aligned to at least eight bytes. It returns `d0`, `d1`, `w1` for the required mask and `w0` for success or refusal. It preserves `d10`, saved registers, the complete `v8` through `v15`, `x27`, SP, FPCR, FPSR and `errno`, with no frame or service call. The parent stores both returned words in its original slots 160 and 168. Refusal changes no result, counter or status and enters the complete original two-call sequence before either result is consumed.

The two logarithm merges retain their original dependency: the first merge's rounded result is the second merge's multiplier. The parent proves zero $`a`$, or negative normal $`a`$ with $`2^{-121}\le\lvert a\rvert<2^{-15}`$, together with $`3/4\le\lvert b\rvert\le19/16`$, $`5/8\le\lvert c\rvert\le7/8`$ and equal signs for $`ab`$ and $`c`$. Their result magnitude stays strictly between one half and one, so its sign and stored exponent remain those of $`c`$. This is a source-wide private precondition; recorded input values do not establish it.

Write $`P=M_aM_b`$, let $`C`$ be the addend significand, and set $`s=2097-e_a-e_b`$ for the stored exponents $`e_a,e_b`$. The exact result magnitude in addend units is $`C+P/2^s`$, with $`67\le s\le173`$. Integer words supply the quotient, first discarded bit and all lower discarded bits. Round upward above halfway, or at halfway when $`C+\lfloor P/2^s\rfloor`$ is odd. Thus exact and halfway cases retain the full nearest-even rule. The merge entry keeps the original `d0`, `d1`, `d2` operand interface and preserves raw `d1` and `d2` on its admitted integer route. ## Interface And Status

The AArch64 application binary interface passes the parent's operands in `d0` and `d1` and returns the result in `d0`. No store reaches the saved-register region. The general arithmetic, fast/wide counter classification and frame sizes stay unchanged. The parent retains the original branches for signed zeros, negative bases, integer exponents, infinities and NaNs, including the original signaling status and payload selection.

# Cubic Spline Piece Coefficients

This child implements the selected `SUVFE_CPSPLINES` calculation from `arpifs/setup/suvfe_cpsplines.F90`. It converts each cubic B-spline into four polynomial coefficients on each of its four supporting knot intervals. A knot is a coordinate where adjacent polynomial pieces meet. The returned centre uses the source Greville definition: the mean of the three interior knots for that basis function. Coordinates, coefficients and the minimum interval length are dimensionless.

`int md2asm_spline_piece_coefficients(const int32_t control[4], const double *minimum_interval, const double bounds[2], const double transform[16], const double knots[basis+4], double pieces[basis*4*4], double centres[basis])` takes seven addresses in x0 through x6 under the Arm procedure call standard. Control contains order, basis count, maximum-search flag and verbose flag, in that order. The admitted values are order four, basis count 21 through 24 or exactly 139 or 140, and both flags zero. The small shapes retain the selected 19-level configuration. The two larger shapes cover the distinct input and output/weight setups of the actual 137-level calculation; other larger counts remain unsupported. Bounds are exact positive zero and one. Minimum interval is finite and nonnegative; its selected value is zero. Inputs and outputs are caller-owned mapped, disjoint buffers. Integer addresses are aligned to four bytes and FP64 addresses to eight bytes.

Transform is the returned matrix of the qualified sampling-transform child. Knots are finite, nondecreasing, clamped to zero and one and admissible under the qualified basis child's multiplicity rules. The parent must establish this condition; the raw percentile knot child alone does not promise basis admissibility for every small scalar tuple. Every consumed arithmetic intermediate is finite. No original module data, derived type, descriptor, vtable or callable is consumed. The explicit control flags preserve the selected source method; unsupported maximum-search or diagnostic modes are rejected rather than silently changed.

Return zero writes every declared piece and centre byte. Return two rejects null addresses, alignment, unsupported controls, bounds or minimum interval before output mutation. Return three propagates a basis-child rejection after computation has started; pieces can then contain zeroes and completed intervals, while centres are not yet written. Valid knot structure, mapped extents and disjointness are caller preconditions. The successful qualified route has no child failure.

Output pieces use the source three-dimensional column-major layout, with basis index varying fastest, then segment, then coefficient. In zero-based indices the element address is `pieces + 8*(basis_index + basis_count*segment + 4*basis_count*coefficient)`. Coefficient zero is the constant term. Every output word first receives positive zero. For each consecutive knot interval, subtract its endpoints and process it only when its absolute length is strictly greater than minimum interval. Generate four local sample positions, convert each to a knot coordinate and evaluate the complete basis through the accepted raw children. For each supported basis function, multiply its four sampled values by the transform. Each coefficient starts at positive zero and accumulates separately rounded products in increasing sample order. Repeated or skipped intervals retain zero coefficients.

After all pieces, compute each centre by adding its three interior knots in increasing source index order and dividing by three. The source's maximum-search and verbose branches are inactive under the explicit control flags. Profiling is omitted diagnostics. Every selected coefficient and centre calculation remains present. Ordinary subtraction, multiplication, addition and division round separately, with no fused instruction or reassociation.

The child retains a 1216-byte frame for the small shapes. The new shapes use a 5856-byte frame: the first 256 bytes hold saved state and sample positions, `[256,1376)` holds the complete basis vector and `[1376,5856)` holds the four-by-basis sample matrix. Both frames are aligned to 16 bytes. The maximum nested frame is 1440 bytes for old shapes or 7008 bytes for the new shapes, including the corresponding basis child. It has no heap allocation. It preserves x18, x19 through x29, the low 64 bits of d8 through d15, FPCR and errno. FPCR is zero. Incoming FPSR bits remain set, and the source arithmetic can add the same numerical flags. No flag or output is repaired after calculation.

# Gaussian Grid Construction Children

The selected T21 grid uses 32 Gaussian latitudes. These latitudes are roots of the degree-32 Legendre polynomial. Two small children build the polynomial coefficients and refine one latitude angle. They reuse the checked Legendre Newton step and direct sine and cosine functions. Initial angle generation, complete grid assembly and SULEGA storage remain separate dependencies.

`md2asm_legendre_fourier_coefficients` receives a writable 17-element FP64 buffer in x0 and the address of the signed 32-bit degree in x1. The degree is 32. It starts the highest Fourier coefficient at two, then for j from one through 32 multiplies it by `sqrt(1-0.25/(j*j))`. Each integer square is formed before exact FP64 conversion. For even j from two through 32, the next lower coefficient is the current coefficient multiplied by `(j-1)*(64-j+2)`, then divided by `j*(64-j+1)`. The numerator and denominator products use exact signed 32-bit arithmetic, then separate FP64 conversion, multiplication and division. Output index16 is the highest coefficient and index0 is the constant coefficient. All 17 values are calculated directly; no table supplies these numerical results.

`md2asm_refine_gaussian_latitude` retains the original seven-pointer GAWL interface: x0 is the 17-element coefficient array, x1 is the angle in radians to update, x2 is the weight to write, x3 is machine epsilon, x4 is the signed 32-bit degree, x5 is the signed 32-bit iteration count to write, and x6 is the last angle correction to update. The degree is 32, epsilon is positive 2**-52, and the finite starting angle lies between zero and pi/2. The coefficient array must already contain the preceding child's calculated values. The caller owns every buffer, which is four-byte aligned for integers or eight-byte aligned for FP64. Distinct input and output buffers do not overlap.

The refinement starts with flag zero and copies the input angle to local storage. Iteration numbers run from one through 21. Each iteration calls the direct CPLEDN child, copies its new angle to the current angle and exits if the call used flag one. Otherwise, when the absolute correction is at most epsilon*1000, it sets flag one for the next call. That next call calculates the weight at the final angle without another Newton update. The routine then copies the final angle and weight to the caller. It preserves the source's extra final weight iteration and maximum-iteration rule.

Both entries use FPCR zero and retain the original FP64 rounding after every operation, including square root. They preserve x18 and all used AAPCS64 saved registers; every stack frame is sixteen-byte aligned. Neither entry allocates storage or calls Fortran, a numerical library, a constructor, a vtable or a type descriptor. The only numerical child of the refinement is the checked direct Newton step. Unsupported degree calls C abort before mutation.
